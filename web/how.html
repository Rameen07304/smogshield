How SmogShield works

SmogShield: four cooperating agents that turn raw air quality data into real-time smog response.

The pipeline

  1. 1

    Monitor

    Fetches and cleans air quality readings.

    Receives
    A city, plus live data from Open-Meteo (or a demo scenario).
    Produces
    The last 6 hourly readings: PM2.5, PM10, the time, and whether they are live or simulated.

  2. 2

    Analysis

    Computes the AQI, the trend and sudden spikes.

    Receives
    The recent readings.
    Produces
    An AQI number, a category (Good to Hazardous), the trend per hour, a spike flag and a one-line reason.

  3. 3

    Advisory

    Turns the assessment into guidance for each audience, in English and اردو.

    Receives
    The assessment: category, trend and spike flag.
    Produces
    A headline and advice for the public, children and schools, the elderly, and people with heart or lung conditions.

  4. 4

    Alert

    Decides when and how to notify people, without spamming them.

    Receives
    The assessment, the advice and the location.
    Produces
    An info, warning or critical alert in the dashboard feed (and Telegram, if set up), or nothing when nothing changed.

Data and honesty

  • Model data, not ground sensors. Readings come from Open-Meteo, based on CAMS (Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service) atmospheric model forecasts. They estimate the air over the surrounding area, not at one street corner.
  • The AQI is an hourly PM2.5 estimate. We apply the US EPA PM2.5 breakpoints to the latest hour. Official AQI uses longer averages, so other sites can show different numbers.
  • Demo scenarios are simulated, and always labelled. A SIMULATED DATA banner, a badge on every screen, and a [SIMULATION] tag on every alert. Simulated alerts are never sent to Telegram unless we switch that on.
  • General guidance, not medical advice. Conservative advice in the style of US EPA AQI messaging, with no medical claims, doses or treatments.

Built to be reliable

  • Fallback to mock data. If the live source fails or takes over 10 seconds, the Monitor switches to clearly labelled simulated data, and to a built-in reading if even that file is missing. The pipeline never stops.
  • Caching. Each city's live data is reused for 10 minutes (the source updates hourly), so refreshing every 5 seconds stays fast and within the free API's limits.
  • Alert cooldown, no spam. One alert when air gets worse, never a repeat for the same level; spikes at most once every 30 minutes; memory resets after 3 hours.
  • Telegram can't break anything. Messages go out in the background with a 3-second timeout. A failure is logged and the alert stays in the dashboard feed; the bot token is never written to logs.
  • No data is never "good". If no valid readings arrive, the level is "unknown" and the advice says to take precautions.
Air quality data: Open-Meteo, based on CAMS ENSEMBLE data (Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service).
